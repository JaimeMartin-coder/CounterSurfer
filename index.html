🐾 The Case of the Counter Surfer
Case #0923 · Urgent

A beagle ate something. Your job is to find out what.

Biscuit, a 3-year-old beagle, jumped up on the kitchen counter while his family was at work. Five foods were sitting out. When his family got home, one food was gone — but everything was knocked over, so nobody knows which one he ate.

The vet team collected a sample from Biscuit's stomach. You are the lab tech. You will use chemical tests to find out which macromolecules are in the sample, then match it to a food.

Why it matters: some human foods are dangerous for dogs. The vet needs your answer fast.

Part 1 Know your suspects

Know your suspects

All food is made of four kinds of macromolecules. Each one is a polymer built from smaller building blocks called monomers. Tap each card to flip it. ✏️ Fill in the molecule table on your sheet.

Checkpoint: match each polymer to its monomer

Flip the cards again and look for the word Monomer on the back. Lipids are the odd one out — they are built from glycerol and fatty acids.
Part 2 Test reference

Train on the tests

A reagent is a chemical that changes when a certain molecule is there. Before you test Biscuit's sample, learn what each test looks like.

For each test, tap Run test to see two results side by side. ✏️ Record the negative and positive results on your sheet.

Three of the tests use reagents. The fourth is the brown paper bag test for lipids (fat or oil): rub a dab on the bag, let it dry, then hold it up to the light. A greasy, see-through spot that stays means lipid. If the spot disappears when dry, it is negative.

Why test distilled water?

Look back at the distilled water sample in each test. It showed the "no" color, so think about which kind of control it is.

Checkpoint: what does "positive" look like?

Look at the known sample for each test — that's what a positive result looks like. The water sample shows negative.
